Resident Evil 2 Remake: A Bestiary of Nightmares

Now let's talk about the MONSTERS of Raccoon City.

⚠️ ALERT! ⚠️ This article contains major spoilers about the story of Resident Evil 2 Remake.

My advice: Do not read this until you've finished the game. You've been warned.

Zombies

First up, the bread and butter: the zombies. In the original, they were clunky but iconic. In this remake, they are absolute nightmares of physics and pressure. These aren't just walking corpses; they're desperate, twitching vectors of infection. The remake nails their durability perfectly—you can pop a headshot and watch them crumble, or you can put six rounds into their chest and watch them keep shambling toward you. The true genius here is the "living death" simulation. Sometimes they play dead on the floor, only to grab your ankle as you walk by. Other times, they shamble fast; other times, agonizingly slow. This unpredictability turns every single dark hallway into a gamble. Do you waste the ammo to guarantee it's down, or do you try to juke past and risk getting a chunk taken out of your neck? That tension is the heart of the game.

My Advice: Shoot them in the head with a shotgun or the Lightning Hawk to make sure they stay down permanently. Aim for the skull, not the chest.

Lickers

If the zombie tests your ammo management, the Licker tests your nerve. The remake took the original's exposed-brain design and cranked the grotesque factor to eleven—rippling muscle, vascular texture, and that horrifically long tongue. But the gameplay twist is what makes them shine. Lickers are completely blind, relying on echolocation. That means you can theoretically walk right past one if you creep slowly. But. The game loves to pair them with explosive turrets or—even better—zombies. Suddenly, you're trapped in a room where you can't shoot the zombie because the gunshot will alert the Licker on the ceiling. You're forced into a deadly game of slow-walking chicken, praying the zombie doesn't spot you first. It's pure survival horror chess.

My Advice: Move slowly when they're nearby. Crouch-walking reduces noise. They have no eyes—only ears. Use that to your advantage.

Tyrant (Mr. X)

Forget the monsters you see; the scariest one is the one you hear. Mr. X isn't just a boss fight; he is a level mechanic with feet. The remake transformed him from a scripted jumpscare into a persistent, AI-driven stalker. Those heavy footsteps echoing through the RPD station create a primal dread that no other enemy can match. He forces you to abandon your meticulous planning. Want to solve that safe puzzle? Too bad—he just walked into the room. Need to grab that key from the locker? You'll have to lead him on a three-floor chase first. He doesn't run, but he doesn't need to. He just keeps walking, shrugging off bullets, forcing you into corners, and compressing your decision-making time into frantic seconds. He turns the police station from a puzzle box into a trap.

My Advice: Similar to Lickers, move slowly. Better yet, stop walking entirely if you can. Hide in a safe room (where you save your game)—he can't follow you there. Use that to catch your breath.

G-Adults

Finally, let's talk about the sewer-dwelling G-Adults. These massive, bulging masses of eyes and bile are the game's way of punishing you for conserving resources in the wrong way. They're slow but possess a terrifying one-hit-kill grab if you get too close. The brilliance here is their placement. The sewers are tight, claustrophobic corridors with barely any room to dodge. You can either waste high-powered shotgun shells or acid rounds to melt them, or you can try to bait their slow charge and squeeze past. But if they grab you, it's either a guaranteed parasite in your inventory (which you have to slash away) or an instant death animation. They represent the body horror of the G-Virus perfectly: an uncontrollable desire to reproduce, creating ugly, weeping tumors that just want to force you to join their mess.

My Advice: Shoot the giant eyeball on their left arm. One well-placed shot staggers them. Two or three kills them. Don't let them hug you.

William Birkin

William Birkin isn't just a boss; he's a tragedy you fight five times. The remake takes his original G-Mutation and turns it into a heartbreaking, body-horror opera. In the beginning, he's almost still human—a desperate scientist who injects his life's work to save himself from a bullet. But by the end of the first sewer chase, he's already slipping. His right arm grows that grotesque, gnashing eye-shoulder, and his entire body language shifts from frantic human to predatory beast. What makes Birkin terrifying isn't just his size or his one-hit-kill attacks—it's the intelligence behind the eyes that slowly erodes. In his first form, he still uses pipes and tactics. By his final transformation, he's just a screaming pile of teeth, claws, and necrotic tissue crawling toward you in the train's emergency lights. The remake adds beautiful, sickening details: the way his eyeball pulsates independently, the wet tearing sound when he rips his own chest apart, and the pathetic grunts he makes when he's stunned. You almost feel bad for the guy. Almost. Then he slams a steel girder through your ribs.

My Advice: Focus fire on his giant eyeballs. Each form has weak points. Grenades and the flamethrower are your best friends. Don't get greedy—he punishes overconfidence.

The Ivy (Plant 43 Zombies)

Forget the zombies. Forget the Lickers. The Plant 43 enemies—called Ivies—are the reason you'll hoard flamethrower fuel like it's gold. These things are former Umbrella researchers who got dosed with a mutated plant strain, and the result is pure botanical nightmare fuel. They don't shamble. They stalk. Their movements are jerky, insectoid, and completely silent except for the wet squelch of vine roots tearing through carpet. The remake's genius redesign made them almost unrecognizable as human—just vaguely humanoid shapes made of green-black vines, with a "head" that splits open like a Venus flytrap to reveal a screaming human jaw still fused inside. Mechanically, they break every rule the game taught you. Shooting them in the head? Useless. They don't have brains anymore. Body shots? They just stagger and keep coming. You need fire. Without it, they'll grab you and perform a "kiss" that injects instant-kill toxins down your throat. Worse, even after you "kill" them, their bodies lie on the floor like harmless piles of weeds… until you walk past, and they reassemble themselves three seconds later. The Ivies teach you one brutal lesson: in the NEST lab, the green stuff wants you dead just as badly as the flesh-eating stuff does.

My Advice: Burn them with fire. The flamethrower is the hard counter. One quick burst makes them flail and die permanently. Without fire, you're just delaying the inevitable.
